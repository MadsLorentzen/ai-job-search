## Tier 1 — Ask if you only get one

**"When you look at the current demand gen surface area — self-serve free-to-paid, Enterprise seat expansion, net-new logo acquisition — where's the biggest gap between what's happening and what you know is possible? What would you already be doing if you had the right person in seat?"**

Why this works: It signals you've mapped the three motions, forces him to reveal his actual priority (not the JD's stated priority), and gives you the real 30/60/90 mandate in his own words. This is the single most valuable question you can ask.

## Tier 2 — Pick one that matches the conversation's direction

**If the conversation went technical/product-led:**  
"How do you think about the handoff point between product-driven acquisition and sales-assisted expansion? Where does the friction show up today — is it in the qualification signal, the seat-level tracking, or the AE motion itself?"

**If the conversation went strategic/positioning:**  
"Perplexity's competitive frame is different depending on the buyer — versus ChatGPT for consumer, versus Glean and Writer for enterprise knowledge, versus Cursor and Claude Code for developer workflows. How do you decide which frame to lead with in demand programs, or do you segment the demand engine by buyer archetype?"

**If the conversation went team/culture:**  
"You've talked about wanting builders who work in first principles rather than playbooks. What does that look like inside your team's weekly rhythm — how do you protect that from becoming reactive to whatever the exec team asked for that week?"

## Tier 3 — The "signal you already did the work" questions

**"Ramp is the most public enterprise case study — 30,000 hours saved across finance ops. What's the internal read on why that landed, and what's the second Ramp — the case study you'd want to be able to publish six months from now?"**

Why this works: You've read the case study. You're asking about narrative strategy, not the case study itself. It tells him you think in terms of proof artifacts and content flywheels.

**"How much of the current pipeline is coming from Perplexity-the-product doing the demand gen work — people trying it, loving it, and pulling it into their company — versus programs your team is running? And which of those two ratios do you want to move?"**

Why this works: This is the core PLG-to-enterprise question. It shows you understand that demand gen at a PLG company isn't about creating demand from zero — it's about instrumenting and accelerating latent demand. If he says "we want more programmatic," you know he wants outbound and ABM muscle. If he says "we want to amplify the product-led signal," you know he wants a growth-marketing-adjacent hire.

## Tier 4 — The closer

Save one of these for the final 2-3 minutes when he asks "any other questions?" the second time:

**"What would make you look back on this hire in twelve months and say 'that was the best decision I made this year'?"**

Why this works: Direct, confident, forces him to articulate the success criteria. His answer becomes your closing statement — you can respond with a one-sentence reflection on how your background maps to what he just described.
