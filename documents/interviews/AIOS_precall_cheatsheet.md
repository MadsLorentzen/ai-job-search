## Referral / Viral Metrics To Know Cold

| Metric | Definition / formula | Why it matters / the lever |
|---|---|---|
| **K-factor (viral coefficient)** | invites sent per user × invite→signup conversion rate | K > 1 = self-sustaining exponential growth. The north star. |
| **30-Day K-Factor** (their target) | same, counted within 30 days of a user joining | 3.6x it by moving invite rate, invite→signup conversion, or compressing cycle time |
| **Viral cycle time** | avg time from a user joining to their invitee joining | Shorter cycle compounds faster; often higher leverage than raw K |
| **Participation rate** | % of eligible users who send ≥1 invite | Top of the referral funnel; trigger timing + prompt placement |
| **Invites per sharer** | avg invites among users who share at all | Channel choice + friction (prefill, SMS vs link vs social) |
| **Invite CTR** | % of invites that get clicked | Sender personalization, social proof, messaging |
| **Visit→signup** | % of referred visitors who create an account | Dedicated referred landing page, incentive clarity |
| **Signup→activation** | % of referred signups who reach first value | Onboarding; tie reward unlock to activation, not signup |
| **Referral rate** | % of new customers attributable to referrals | The headline "how much growth is organic" number |
| **Referral CAC** | (incentives + program cost) ÷ referred customers | Compare to paid CAC; usually the cheap channel |
| **LTV referred vs non-referred** | retention/value of referred cohort vs rest | Referred users usually retain better — the quiet superpower, name it |
| **Reward redemption rate** | % of earned rewards actually claimed | Incentive design; unredeemed = friction or weak incentive |
| **Fraud / gaming rate** | % of referrals flagged as abuse | Critical in cash-incentive *health* referrals (and compliance) |

### The referral loop, step by step (and the lever at each)

1. **Trigger / eligibility** — prompt at the right "aha" moment (post-activation, first result). *Lever: timing.*
2. **Prompt / exposure** — the CTA surface, copy, frequency. *Lever: placement.*
3. **Share / invite sent** — channel + friction. *Metric: participation rate, invites per sharer. Lever: prefill, reduce steps.*
4. **Delivery / landing** — invite arrives and is seen. *Lever: channel deliverability.*
5. **Click / visit** — *Metric: invite CTR. Lever: social proof, sender name.*
6. **Signup** — *Metric: visit→signup. Lever: dedicated referred LP, clear double-sided incentive.*
7. **Activation** — referred user reaches value. *Metric: signup→activation. Lever: onboarding + unlock tied to activation.*
8. **Reward** — both sides paid. *Metric: redemption. Lever: incentive design + fraud control.*
9. **Re-loop** — new user becomes a referrer. *Metric: time-to-first-invite for the new cohort → this drives cycle time. Lever: prompt new users early.*

**One-liner to have ready:** "To move 30-day K by 3.6x I'd decompose it into invite rate, invite→signup conversion, and cycle time, then attack whichever has the worst leverage ratio first — usually it's participation and cycle time, not the incentive size everyone reaches for."

## Answering the Two Hard Questions

### "What are your salary expectations?"
You've been advised not to quote a number first or early, and that's right for this phase. The band is already public, so naming a number inside it gains you nothing and burns your ceiling. Make them go first.

**Lead with this (deflect + question):**
> "I'm comfortable in the band you've published, so I don't think we'll have a problem there. Before I put a number on it, I'd want to understand the full scope and where you see this role landing in terms of level. How are you thinking about the band for this particular hire?"

**Only if pushed a second time ("ballpark, where are you?"):** give a direction, not a figure. Don't dodge twice, that reads as evasive.
> "Toward the upper end of what you've published, given the scope and that I've built these systems hands-on. I'd rather get precise once we both know it's a fit."

That plants the anchor high without spending it. One clean deflection, then one directional answer if pushed, then move on. Know your private walkaway floor; never say it out loud. (The number-naming move comes later, at offer stage, with leverage.)

### "Why did you leave Checkmate?"
Lead with impact, state the cut flatly, let the contrast do the work. You can't say "good terms" and you won't, but you also won't air the grievance. A peer-level operator who hears "drove the inflection" then "and they cut the role" supplies the *that's their loss* on their own. The whole spine is: I joined for the B2B engine, I was integral to the inflection, and when they pivoted away from experimentation-led growth they wound down the function. Throughline = I follow the leverage.

**Lead with this (~35 sec):**
> "I joined Checkmate for the B2B side of their B2B2C vision. The pure B2C play wasn't the draw; the engine they were building underneath it was. When the B2B motion took off I was in the center of it. I pushed for an enrichment product that went from zero to 15% of total revenue in about ten weeks, ran the experiments on targeting, their highest-revenue surface, and rebuilt the growth stack: brought in a growth engineer and got us to eight clean experiments a week. That work was a big part of cracking their B2B2C flywheel. At year-end they decided to go all-in on sales-led B2B, which wound down the experimentation-driven growth function I'd built, and they cut those roles, mine included. Since then I've gone deep on AI and only look at AI-native roles now, which is exactly why this one caught my attention."

**Back pocket, only if they probe "why you specifically?":**
> "The stated reason was flat B2C numbers, but B2C was about 10% of where I spent my time, and I'd doubled its LTV even as a side priority. The real driver was the structural shift to sales-led B2B. It wasn't just my role; they wound down the growth function."

This quietly closes the only thing they actually worry about (fired for cause / performance) by framing it as structural, and the doubled-LTV stat dismantles their rationale without you calling it unfair.

**The one risk is tone, not words.** The script is clean; residual frustration in your voice isn't. Deliver the exit beat flat and slightly bored, like old news you're fully past. Say it out loud a few times until it carries no charge.

**Proof points to have loaded** (these show you were integral to the inflection, not adjacent to it): enrichment 0 → 15% of revenue in ~10 weeks · owned experiments on targeting (highest-revenue surface) · rebuilt the growth stack · brought in a growth engineer, 8 clean experiments/week · helped crack the B2B2C flywheel · doubled B2C LTV as a ~10% side priority. Spread across both B2B and B2C for socials, SEO, events, lifecycle, ads, ABM, content with one marketer and no budget.
