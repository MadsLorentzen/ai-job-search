### The four eras of B2B demand gen

1. **MQL-factory era (~2006–2018).** Marketo/HubSpot-driven lead scoring. Success = volume of Marketing Qualified Leads passed to sales. Funnel-shaped, linear, lead-centric.
2. **Account-based era (~2016–2021).** SiriusDecisions/Terminus/6sense pushed toward targeting named accounts and buying committees instead of individual leads. ABM tiers (1:1, 1:few, 1:many) become standard vocabulary.
3. **Intent-data era (~2019–2023).** Third-party intent signals (Bombora, G2, TechTarget) layered onto ABM to time outreach. "Signal-based selling" emerges as a category term.
4. **AI-native era (2023–2026).** LLM-driven personalization at scale, AI SDRs/agents, programmatic outbound (Clay-style data enrichment + AI writing), dark-funnel/dark-social measurement problems. Broad rejection of the MQL as primary success metric in favor of buyer-group and signal-based models.
### The new GTM operating model

Every credible GTM voice in the research converges on the same shape ([Chris Walker](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/chriswalker171_revenue-attribution-sales-activity-7138525823707320320-5-cn); [Kyle Poyar / Growth Unhinged](https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/2025-state-of-b2b-gtm-report); [Sangram Vajre / GTM Partners](https://gtmpartners.com/); [Jason Lemkin / SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/)):
- **Owned, high-quality artifacts** (Spaces, changelogs, research reports, templates) engineered for AI discoverability, not just Google.
- **Individual-brand distribution** — founders, engineers, and functional experts posting under their own names, not the company page.
- **Signal-triggered, human-in-the-loop outbound** replacing volume sequences.
- **Product-led loops** measured by trial-to-first-value, not MQL count.
- **Consumption / outcome pricing** alongside or replacing per-seat pricing.
- **Incrementality testing** (geo, holdout, matched cohort, MMM) replacing multi-touch attribution.    
- **Developer or community substrate** — the durable moat that compounds regardless of channel volatility.

### Attribution models

|Model|Mechanic|Best fit|
|---|---|---|
|First-touch|100% credit to first interaction|Simple top-of-funnel channel comparison|
|Last-touch|100% credit to final interaction before conversion|Simple, but systematically overweights bottom-funnel/branded-search channels|
|Multi-touch (linear)|Equal credit across all touches|Fairer but ignores stage importance|
|**U-shaped** (position-based)|Heavy credit to first touch + conversion touch, light middle|Common recommendation for 50–250 employee B2B teams|
|**W-shaped**|Heavy credit to first touch, lead-creation touch, and opportunity-creation touch|Popular in B2B because it credits the three moments sales/marketing care about most|
|Data-driven / algorithmic|Model-fit credit based on observed conversion paths|Requires volume; degrading in accuracy|
|**Marketing Mix Modeling (MMM)**|Aggregate econometric modeling — no individual-level tracking required|Ascendant in 2025–2026 as user-level attribution degrades|

An estimated **64% of B2B organizations lack a formal UTM tagging policy** (Gartner via aggregator sources), which is one of the underappreciated reasons attribution data quality is poor — the modeling choice matters less than the input hygiene.

3.8 The 2025–2026 attribution shift: why MMM is back

Multi-touch attribution depends on stitching together individual-level journeys, and that capability has been degrading for years due to third-party cookie deprecation, Apple's App Tracking Transparency, and general signal loss. MTA now sees an estimated **30–60% of actual touchpoints** at best.

Marketing Mix Modeling — aggregate, privacy-proof, econometric — doesn't need individual tracking. Google open-sourced its **Meridian** MMM tool (Bayesian causal inference) in early 2025, and Meta's **Robyn** offers a comparable open-source package, collapsing MMM's traditional six-figure-consulting-engagement cost of entry.

In a TransUnion survey reported by eMarketer, **46.9% of US brand and agency marketers plan to invest in MMM** over the next year, and **27.6% now name it their single most reliable measurement method** — ahead of multi-touch attribution's 19.4% ([The Matchbox](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.thematchbox.inc%2Fresources%2Fmarketing-mix-modeling-guide)). The IAB published a vendor-neutral "Modernizing MMM" best-practice guide in December 2025.

**The 2026 consensus is triangulation, not replacement:** MMM for strategic budget allocation, incrementality testing (geo/holdout experiments) as the causal ground-truth layer, and MTA retained only for tactical day-to-day, in-platform optimization where tracking still functions. eMarketer separately reports **71% of brands have reduced reliance on user-level data** as of 2025.

### The shift from lead-based to account/buyer-group metrics

Because Forrester's own data shows 95% of purchases involve 3+ people across 2+ departments, lead-level scoring systematically undercounts real buying activity — five different people from the same account each triggering a "new lead" record fragments what is actually one deal.

The fix practitioners are converging on: score and route at the **account** or **buying-group** level (aggregate engagement across all known contacts at a target account) rather than the individual-lead level.

### Signal and fit scoring

|Score type|What it measures|Common inputs|
|---|---|---|
|**ICP fit score**|How closely a company matches ideal customer profile|Firmographic (size, industry, tech stack), sometimes psychographic|
|**Intent score**|Likelihood a company is actively in-market|Third-party intent data (Bombora, G2), search behavior, content consumption|
|**Engagement score**|Depth/recency of interaction with owned properties|Website visits, email opens, content downloads, event attendance|

The 2025–2026 critique: third-party intent-data precision against actual buyer behavior is estimated at only **30–40%** ([Leadgen Economy](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.leadgen-economy.com%2Fblog%2Fai-sdr-cancellation-wave-failure-forensics%2F)), driving the shift toward first-party product-usage and community signals as more trustworthy inputs.

### Product-led metrics

|Metric|Definition|
|---|---|
|**PQL**|Individual user crossing a usage threshold that predicts conversion|
|**PQA**|Account-level aggregate of PQL-qualified users (commonly 50%+ threshold)|
|**Activation rate**|% of signups reaching a defined "aha moment"/core-value action|
|**Time-to-value (TTV)**|Elapsed time from signup to first realized value|
|**Expansion signal**|Usage crossing a seat/tier threshold (e.g., 80% of licensed seats), triggering an automated upsell motion|

A recommended operational trigger from current PLG playbooks: when usage exceeds ~80% of licensed seats or a new business unit is detected inside an account, auto-create a CRM expansion opportunity and notify the AE/CSM rather than waiting for a renewal conversation ([Prospeo](https://www.google.com/url?sa=E&q=https%3A%2F%2Fprospeo.io%2Fs%2Fland-and-expand-strategy)).

### The highest-leverage first bets

Ranked roughly by (a) how directly they solve the core asymmetry in Section 6.1, and (b) how buildable they are with tools/signals that already exist in Perplexity's stack today.

#### Bet 1 — Build an account-level expansion scoring model on the existing self-serve base

**Why first:** This is the single highest-leverage lever because it doesn't require generating any new top-of-funnel demand — it requires organizing demand that already exists. Given tens of millions of users and 50,000+ organizations already touching the product, even a rough v1 scoring model (domain-match + seat density + growth rate + persona mix) run against the existing user base would likely surface hundreds of qualified expansion accounts sales isn't currently working.

This directly answers the JD's language about "downstream conversion to revenue" and dovetails with the Sales Systems Engineer's explicit mandate.

**Concrete v1 scope:** Partner with the Sales Systems Engineer role to define the scoring logic (own the business rules; they own the pipeline/infrastructure). Ship a ranked account list to the enterprise sales team within the first month. Instrument conversion of scored accounts vs. unscored outbound as the validation metric.

#### Bet 2 — Signal-based outbound targeting Pro-user-dense accounts (the JD's literal ask)

**Why:** This is quite literally what "architect and operate a modern demand engine using AI-native tooling — programmatic outbound, AI-personalized sequences, intent-based targeting" means in the JD, applied to the highest-quality intent signal Perplexity has: **existing product usage inside a named account**, which is a materially stronger intent signal than third-party intent data (Bombora, 6sense).

Build (not buy) AI-personalized outbound sequences referencing the account's actual usage pattern (e.g., "your team has been running X Deep Research queries per week on [topic] — here's what an Enterprise rollout would unlock") — a level of personalization competitors without Perplexity's own usage data cannot easily replicate.

**Tooling already visible in adjacent job postings:** Apollo, Clay, Unify, LinkedIn Sales Navigator (from the Lead Gen Specialist JD); Salesforce, Snowflake, Workato, Hightouch/Census (from the Systems Engineer JD).

#### Bet 3 — Formalize the "shadow-IT-to-Enterprise" motion for Comet specifically

**Why:** Comet Enterprise is new (March 2026), directly IT-buyer-facing, and — per the CrowdStrike partnership and 500+ policy controls — solves a real governance anxiety for security teams watching employees already use the free Comet browser. This is a faster, warmer expansion motion than net-new logo generation, and ties demand gen directly to a flagship 2026 product launch leadership clearly wants adopted at scale.

**Concrete v1 scope:** Identify accounts with high Comet consumer/Pro usage inside domains that also have an existing Perplexity Enterprise contract (upsell) or high seat count without one (net-new). Build a governance-anxiety-focused campaign (case studies, security one-pagers) modeled on the Ramp case study's IT-buyer-reassurance framing.

#### Bet 4 — Turn the Business Fellowship and Ramp-style case studies into a repeatable ABM content engine

**Why:** The Business Fellowship already recruits senior GTM/strategy leaders globally (mentors include Jensen Huang, Aaron Levie, Ali Ghodsi) and grants a free Enterprise Pro subscription to their company. Structurally, this is a lead-gen and land-and-expand mechanism, but nothing in public material suggests it's being tracked or attributed as a demand gen channel with ARR influence.

Similarly, the Ramp case study is exactly the artifact type (named GTM leader, named productivity number, security-buyer-credible) that should be systematically produced for 3–5 more accounts per quarter and distributed through ABM/content channels the JD names, rather than existing as a one-off.

**Concrete v1 scope:** Audit current Fellowship cohort companies for expansion-candidate scoring (Bet 1's model applied to a known, high-intent list). Propose ARR-attribution instrumentation for the Fellowship as a formal channel. Commission 2–3 new case studies specifically targeting IT/security buyer personas.

#### Bet 5 — Partnership-driven demand: enable the resellers rather than out-market them

**Why:** SoftBank's 7,000-person enterprise sales team and Databricks/Snowflake/AWS co-sell motions are effectively pre-built demand gen and sales capacity Perplexity doesn't have to build — but likely needs enablement content, joint campaigns, and lead-qualification criteria to convert efficiently. Lower-effort, faster-payback than building net-new international campaigns from scratch, and matches the JD's explicit mention of "partnerships" as a channel.

**Concrete v1 scope:** Inventory existing partner enablement assets (or lack thereof) for SoftBank, AWS, Snowflake, Databricks. Ship a co-sell one-pager and qualification rubric for at least one partner within 60 days.
